# Track profiles

Read the section matching the selected track. These profiles change emphasis, interaction, pacing, and task design; they do not replace the actual student profile.

## Primary school

- Use short 5–12 minute activity cycles and concrete Chinese explanations; long teacher monologues are especially ineffective for this age group.
- Determine whether the task is ordinary primary English or KET/A2 Key preparation. KET work should still feel age-appropriate rather than like a reduced high-school reading lesson.
- Limit the first-pass vocabulary set to 8–12 essential words or chunks. Recycle each target through picture recognition, listening/pointing, oral recall, spelling, collocation, and one sentence.
- Teach the passage paragraph by paragraph: predict from the picture, hear/read the English, chunk the sentence, explain it in Chinese, locate evidence, then ask for a short oral response.
- Give every visible paragraph a natural Chinese translation in the teaching materials. Keep the English prominent and use the Chinese to confirm meaning, not to replace reading.
- Prefer visible progress: predict, read aloud, point to evidence, sequence, classify fact/fiction, retell, and create one new sentence.
- Keep the PPT image-led and reveal answers only after the student responds.
- Build practice and homework in the visual progression shown in the user's sample: picture-word recognition → copy/spell → matching → collocation/choice/fill → reading evidence → “今天我会了吗？” self-check.
- Read [primary-ket-workflow.md](primary-ket-workflow.md) before generating any primary/KET teacher script, PPT, practice, or homework.

## Junior high

- Combine vocabulary and grammar with paragraph gist, evidence locating, and sentence-level production.
- Connect explanations to the student's school curriculum and current test patterns without turning every passage into pure test drilling.
- Use a guided example, a paired attempt with the teacher, and an independent attempt.

## Senior high

- Emphasize sentence structure, reference words, discourse logic, paragraph purpose, evidence, paraphrase, and distractor analysis.
- Add timed work only after the student can explain the reasoning accurately.
- Use transfer practice with a new sentence or short paragraph rather than repeating the same item form.

## CET-4 and CET-6

- Diagnose whether the bottleneck is vocabulary coverage, parsing speed, discourse recognition, evidence locating, or time allocation.
- Teach word families, collocations, paraphrase recognition, and efficient passage navigation.
- Separate accuracy practice from timed practice and report both.
- CET-6 should use denser arguments and subtler paraphrase than CET-4.

## New Concept English

- Confirm the book and lesson number, then work from the user's licensed excerpt.
- For New Concept 3, connect vocabulary, collocations, grammar, long-sentence parsing, paragraph logic, translation choices, retelling, and writing transfer.
- Do not make memorization the only outcome. Use the text as a reusable language model.
- Preserve the wording of the provided source when quoting it, and distinguish quotation from teacher-created examples.

## IELTS Academic Reading

- Map each item to its official question type and teach the specific skill it tests.
- Build paraphrase pairs and an answer-and-evidence map.
- For True/False/Not Given and Yes/No/Not Given, explicitly separate contradiction from absence of information and prohibit outside-knowledge answers.
- Treat technical background as a comprehension aid; IELTS passages are written for non-specialists.
- Include spelling, word-limit, and answer-transfer checks where relevant.

## TOEFL iBT before 21 January 2026

- Use the legacy passage-and-question structure only when the test date or supplied material clearly belongs to it.
- Focus on vocabulary in context, factual information, inference, rhetorical purpose, sentence insertion, prose summary, passage organization, and academic reading speed as applicable to the supplied material.

## TOEFL iBT from 21 January 2026

- Recognize the multistage adaptive Reading section and do not promise a fixed item count for an individual test form.
- Support the current Reading task families: Complete the Words, Read in Daily Life, and Read an Academic Passage.
- Teach both academic reading and practical digital-text reading; do not convert every task into a legacy 700-word passage lesson.
- Use the current 1–6 section scale when discussing targets. During the transition period, mention a 0–120 comparison only when it helps the student understand an existing requirement.
- Verify current structure against ETS when the request concerns test format rather than a supplied practice item.

## Cross-track adaptation rule

Change the cognitive load, explanation depth, interaction frequency, visual density, and independence expected—not merely the font size or number of vocabulary words.
