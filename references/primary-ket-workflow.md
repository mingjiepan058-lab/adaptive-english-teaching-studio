# Primary and KET/A2 Key workflow

Use this reference for upper-primary reading and KET/A2 Key materials.

## Teaching sequence for a 120-minute one-to-one lesson

Use short cycles rather than long explanation blocks:

| Time | Activity | Visible success check |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 min | picture prediction and lesson goal | names the topic or two animals |
| 8–25 min | 8–12 essential picture words | points to, reads, and recalls the words |
| 25–45 min | first paragraph guided reading | reads chunks and gives the main idea |
| 45–60 min | second paragraph and evidence | answers a detail question with evidence |
| 60–65 min | short break | — |
| 65–85 min | remaining text / fact-or-fiction | classifies each claim and explains why |
| 85–100 min | language patterns and collocations | completes three short patterns |
| 100–113 min | independent classroom practice | completes a short mixed worksheet |
| 113–120 min | self-check and assignment briefing | rates three can-do statements |

Change the exact split when the material is shorter or longer, but keep the total at 120 minutes.

## Passage handling

For each visible paragraph:

1. Preserve the English source text accurately.
2. Split it into child-readable chunks for oral reading.
3. Provide a natural Chinese translation.
4. Select only the essential words/chunks needed to understand it.
5. Ask one gist or evidence question.
6. Reuse one sentence pattern in a new child-friendly example.

Do not invent text cut off by the image or missing from the supplied pages.

## Vocabulary selection

Prioritize high-utility and passage-critical items. For an animal-facts article, useful categories include:

- animal names: chimpanzee, orangutan, koala, elephant, bull
- topic nouns: creature, fingerprint, pattern, microscope, myth
- evidence verbs/chunks: collect, look closely, tell the difference, be similar to, charge at
- describing words: weird, unusual, ridged, colour-blind

Teach no more than 8–12 as primary targets in one lesson. Treat the rest as recognition words.

Every primary target should appear in at least three forms across the package: picture/meaning, oral or spelling retrieval, and a collocation or sentence.

## Sample-inspired worksheet contract

Use the user's P1/P2 examples as a learning-flow reference, not as a visual template to copy.

The student worksheet should usually contain:

1. **先看图：我会认这些词** — picture + English + short Chinese cue
2. **小侦探拼写** — missing letters or write from a picture
3. **连一连 / 圈一圈** — picture-word or fact-feature matching
4. **搭配小积木** — choose the correct word/chunk for a phrase
5. **读文章，找证据** — 3–5 short comprehension items
6. **今天我会了吗？** — three concrete can-do statements and a retry checkbox

Avoid too many task types on one page. Use large type, generous white space, friendly headings, and answer targets large enough for handwriting.

## PPT contract

- Use an image-led title and a clear “Strange or true?” hook.
- Reveal no more than 8–12 target words, in small groups.
- Show one short paragraph per reading slide, followed by its Chinese translation.
- Use click/reveal sequencing conceptually: question first, answer and evidence on the next slide.
- Teach only useful grammar patterns from the text, such as `There are...`, `be similar to`, `cannot + verb`, `such as`, `because`, and past-simple event verbs.
- End with a visible can-do check matching the worksheet.

## Teacher script contract

Use natural Chinese classroom wording. Every activity block should contain:

- the exact teacher line
- the English word/sentence to model
- the question to ask
- expected answer
- one hint if the student struggles
- one extension if the student answers quickly
- slide and worksheet reference

Do not script two hours of uninterrupted speech. The learner should respond, point, read, choose, or explain at least once every 3–5 minutes.
