# Deliverable specifications

## Teacher verbatim script

Create an editable `teacher-script.docx` as a runnable lesson, not a prose essay. Use a table or clearly repeated blocks containing:

- timestamp and slide number
- teaching intention
- **Teacher says**: natural Chinese wording with necessary English examples
- **Teacher asks**: a specific question
- **Expected response**: acceptable student answers
- **If the student struggles**: one hint, simpler explanation, or fallback example
- **Teacher reminder**: pacing, board work, pronunciation, or common error
- transition to the next activity

Allow brief optional branches for a stronger or weaker response. Do not script fake student dialogue word for word.

## PPTX

Create a real editable `lesson-slides.pptx` in 16:9 format. Use Chinese for explanations and retain English source language. Aim for a clear visual hierarchy, large readable type, and one learning purpose per slide.

The deck should normally include:

1. lesson goal and success check
2. quick diagnostic or prediction
3. essential vocabulary/chunks
4. just-enough background or concept visual when needed
5. passage structure or story map
6. guided sentence/paragraph analysis
7. question strategy with evidence mapping
8. student attempt slides without answers
9. reveal-and-explain slides after the attempt
10. recap, retrieval check, and homework briefing

Use visuals that explain the topic rather than decorative stock imagery. Keep full teacher wording out of the slides. Put source credits in a discreet footer or final source slide.

For primary/KET, show one short paragraph or one small task at a time. Pair every visible source paragraph with a natural Chinese translation, teach essential vocabulary through picture cards, and include simple language patterns from the text. Use the source's fact/fiction, story, or information structure as the main interaction rather than adding unrelated games.

## In-class practice

Create student-facing `class-practice.docx` plus `class-practice-answer-key.docx`.

Use three layers when the source supports them:

1. guided retrieval of vocabulary, chunks, or sentence structure
2. evidence-based comprehension or target question type
3. transfer to a new sentence, short paragraph, retelling, or analogous item

Sequence items to match the lesson. Include estimated time and point value or success criteria. The answer key should give brief reasoning and passage evidence, not just letters.

For primary/KET, follow the sample-inspired worksheet order when it fits the material:

1. 看图认词：picture cards with English and concise Chinese meaning
2. 描写/拼写：copy, missing-letter, or listen-and-write tasks
3. 连线/分类：picture-word, animal-feature, or fact/fiction matching
4. 搭配小积木：choose a verb/chunk and complete a short phrase or sentence
5. 阅读证据：short multiple choice, true/false, sequencing, or “圈出证据” tasks
6. 今天我会了吗：three concrete can-do statements with “很棒 / 还可以 / 再练一次”

Use friendly section titles and generous response space. The student version must not show answers, translations that directly reveal tested items, or teacher-only prompts.

## Homework

Create `homework.docx` plus `homework-answer-key.docx`. Target 20–40 minutes unless the user specifies otherwise.

Include:

- a short retrieval component from the lesson
- one closely related application task
- one small transfer task that reveals whether the student can use the idea independently
- submission instructions and estimated time
- answer key or rubric for every gradable task

Do not duplicate the class worksheet. Reduce volume for primary learners, keep directions understandable without a teacher present, and include a brief reading-aloud or retelling task when suitable.
